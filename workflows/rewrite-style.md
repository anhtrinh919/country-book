# Rewrite style — Country Book, Round 2

The reader is Momo, a curious 7-year-old in Vietnam. A parent may read aloud. Every sentence must make sense to him on first hearing.

## Voice

- Short sentences. Aim for about 10 words. Never more than 20.
- One idea per sentence. Split "X, and Y, which Z" into two or three sentences.
- Active verbs. "Tigers hunt in the forest", not "The forest is home to tigers".
- Everyday words a 7-year-old knows. Explain any hard word in the same sentence: "silt (rich mud)".
- Concrete pictures over labels. "Snow covers the mountains", not "snow-capped peaks".
- Talk to the reader sometimes: "Can you guess…?", "Imagine…". At most once per section.
- Cut about a fifth of the words. Drop filler, not facts. A page holds at most 380 words in all.
- Each section has two paragraphs side by side. Keep them about the same length.
- British spelling stays as it is (colour, metre).

## Banned AI phrases

Never use: nestled, boasts, tapestry, renowned, vibrant, iconic, remarkable, breathtaking, stunning, rich (as in "rich history"), unique, diverse, fiercely, mighty, treasure (as a verb), "home to", "is known for", "a land of", "from X to Y" openers, "whether you…", "it's no wonder", "truly", "steeped in". No em-dash chains — at most one dash per paragraph.

## Facts

- Keep every fact, name, number, and date exactly. Do not round, add, or drop numbers in the main text.
- You may drop a minor detail to make room. Never change one.
- Do not touch: facts[], words[], photos, flag, theme, motif, islands, name, nativeName, region, coords, fileNo, langName.

## Fields you rewrite

tagline (≤28 chars), sections.* (≤30 chars each, playful), lead (≤340 chars), geo[2] (≤320), animals[2] (≤300), culture[2] (≤330), history[2] (≤280), wows[7] (≤110, one sentence each), myths[2] (myth ≤60, fact ≤175).

Keep the same number of items in every list. Keep sections.wows as "7 things to make you go wow" or similar.

## Myths

Each myth box must teach something new. A myth fails if its answer repeats anything on the page: lead, geo, animals, culture, history, wows, or the six facts.

- First check the old myth. If its answer repeats the page, replace the myth.
- A good myth is a belief a child (or adult) really holds. Example: "Penguins live at the North Pole."
- The answer corrects it with a short, surprising, true fact. Use only well-known, certain facts. If unsure, choose a different myth.
- Never wrap the myth in quote marks. The page adds them.

## Example — Myanmar lead

Before: "Myanmar sits at the heart of Southeast Asia, where the Irrawaddy River flows past thousands of golden pagodas to the sea. From snowy peaks in the north to coral islands in the south, it holds ancient temples, rare wildlife, and dozens of peoples — each with their own language, dress, and festivals."

After: "Myanmar is in Southeast Asia, next to Thailand and India. A giant river called the Irrawaddy runs right through it. Thousands of golden temples sit along its banks. Snow covers the mountains in the north. Coral islands dot the warm sea in the south. More than 100 groups of people live here, and each has its own clothes and festivals."

## Vietnamese

Translate from the new English, not the old Vietnamese. Write as a Vietnamese children's book would: câu ngắn, từ ngữ quen thuộc, giọng kể thân thiện. Keep the same facts, numbers, and item counts. Keep each string about as long as the English, and never more than 1.2 times its length. Always call the reader "em", never "bạn". Use common Vietnamese names for places and animals where they exist (e.g. "sông Irrawaddy", "voi châu Á"). Do not copy myth answers from the page text — the same uniqueness rule applies.
